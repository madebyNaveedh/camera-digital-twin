\section{System Overview \& Architecture}
This digital twin links a physical desk observation area to a 3D virtual simulation in \textbf{CoppeliaSim}. A real camera observes physical markers or colored objects, estimates their coordinates in real time, synchronises their state into an open $3.0\text{m} \times 3.0\text{m}$ virtual arena, and drives a simulated \textbf{Pioneer P3DX} mobile robot that navigates autonomously while reacting safely to obstacle introductions.

The pipeline executes asynchronously at \textbf{30 FPS} across four dedicated stages:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Physical Sensing:} A Logitech USB webcam captures RGB frames ($640\times480$ @ 30 FPS) via Linux V4L2.
    \item \textbf{Perception Engine:} Extracts object centroid $(u_t, v_t)$ in pixel space with sub-millisecond execution.
    \item \textbf{Spatial Transformation:} Maps pixel coordinates to simulation coordinates $(X_{\text{sim}}, Y_{\text{sim}})$ with natural desk sensitivity.
    \item \textbf{Virtual Twin Synchronisation:} Updates the virtual obstacle in CoppeliaSim via ZeroMQ Remote API ($<15\text{ ms}$ latency).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Real-Time Perception Engine}
To guarantee deterministic, high-speed tracking without neural network inference overhead, two perception modes are supported:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{ArUco Fiducial Marker Tracking:} Detects predefined \texttt{DICT\_4X4\_50} markers. Four high-contrast corners are resolved to sub-pixel accuracy, computing the geometric centroid with high immunity to ambient illumination changes.
    \item \textbf{Three-Color Presets (HSV):} Provides three clean, mutually exclusive color filters tailored to common objects:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Blue Preset [Key 1]:} $H \in [100, 135]$, $S \in [100, 255]$, $V \in [60, 255]$
        \item \textbf{Green Preset [Key 2]:} $H \in [35, 85]$, $S \in [70, 255]$, $V \in [50, 255]$
        \item \textbf{Red Preset [Key 3]:} Wraps hue boundary ($H \in [0, 10] \cup [168, 180]$, $S \in [100, 255]$, $V \in [70, 255]$)
    \end{itemize}
    Binary masks are filtered with morphological opening and closing ($5\times5$ ellipse) to eliminate salt-and-pepper noise before computing spatial moments ($c_u = M_{10}/M_{00}, c_v = M_{01}/M_{00}$).
\end{itemize}

\section{Bilinear Coordinate Mapping \& Sensitivity Scaling}
The physical camera monitors an active desk tracking zone:
\begin{equation}
    u \in [u_{\text{min}}, u_{\text{max}}] = [100, 540]\text{ px}, \quad v \in [v_{\text{min}}, v_{\text{max}}] = [60, 420]\text{ px}
\end{equation}
Raw coordinates are normalized $\bar{u}, \bar{v} \in [-1.0, 1.0]$. A sensitivity gain ($G = 1.0$) maps natural desk hand movements directly across the $3.0\text{m} \times 3.0\text{m}$ virtual arena ($X, Y \in [-1.4\text{m}, +1.4\text{m}]$), preventing the object from inadvertently exiting the camera frame. An Exponential Moving Average (EMA) filter eliminates high-frequency hand jitter:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{p}_{\text{filtered}}[t] = \alpha \cdot \mathbf{p}_{\text{raw}}[t] + (1 - \alpha) \cdot \mathbf{p}_{\text{filtered}}[t-1], \quad \alpha = 0.70
\end{equation}
A state-holding watchdog preserves the last known 3D position for $2.0\text{ seconds}$ if the physical object leaves the camera frame.

All interior crossbars and divider walls were removed, leaving an unobstructed patrol path. \subsection{Autonomous Square Perimeter Patrol}
The differential-drive \textbf{Pioneer P3DX} ($R = 0.0975\text{m}$, $L = 0.381\text{m}$) navigates a continuous square circuit around the arena perimeter across four waypoints:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{W}_0 (+0.95, -0.95) \longrightarrow \mathbf{W}_1 (+0.95, +0.95) \longrightarrow \mathbf{W}_2 (-0.95, +0.95) \longrightarrow \mathbf{W}_3 (-0.95, -0.95)
\end{equation}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Perimeter Cruising:} Linear forward velocity $v = 0.16\text{ m/s}$ with proportional yaw steering ($k_p = 0.85$).
    \item \textbf{Zero-Radius Corner Turning:} Upon reaching a corner waypoint ($d < 0.16\text{m}$), the robot halts linear motion ($v = 0.0\text{ m/s}$) and rotates in place ($\omega = 0.60\text{ rad/s}$) until aligned with the next leg. This eliminates wide turn radii that would otherwise clip boundary walls.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Dual-Layer Reactive Collision Avoidance}
The Pioneer robot responds autonomously to physical obstacle manipulations via a dual-layer safety mechanism:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Virtual Heading Cone Evaluation:} The controller continuously measures the relative vector between the robot and virtual obstacle. If the obstacle is within $0.70\text{m}$ and inside a frontal heading cone of $\pm 50^\circ$, wheel velocities immediately halt with status \texttt{OBSTACLE BLOCKING PATH}. Once the physical object is moved away, the robot smoothly resumes its perimeter loop.
    \item \textbf{Frontal Ultrasonic Clearance Check:} Eight onboard Pioneer ultrasonic sensors are polled in real time. If any reading drops below $0.28\text{m}$, the robot halts with status \texttt{WALL CLEARANCE HALT}.
    \item \textbf{Anti-Fall Watchdog:} If the robot height drops ($Z < 0.05\text{m}$) or exceeds bounds, the supervisor automatically recovers and re-positions the robot safely at waypoint $\mathbf{W}_0$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
This camera-driven digital twin demonstrates physical-to-virtual state synchronisation. By combining calibrated desk coordinate mapping with robust ArUco and three-color perception, physical obstacle movements reliably command virtual obstacles in CoppeliaSim, driving responsive, collision-free autonomous navigation in the Pioneer P3DX mobile robot.
